For many business owners, email is the first thing they check in the morning and the last thing they check at night. It is where new clients arrive, where problems surface and where most of the day's small decisions wait. It is also one of the easiest places to lose hours without noticing.

The answer is not to ignore email. It is to put a clear system around it, one that someone else can run on your behalf. Here is a system we like because it is simple, it works with any email provider and it is easy to hand over.

## The goal: you only see what needs you

A good inbox system does not aim for an empty inbox for its own sake. It aims for one thing: every message is dealt with by the right person, at the right time, and you only spend time on the emails that genuinely need your attention.

To get there, every incoming email goes into one of five categories.

## The five categories

### 1. Reply now

Simple messages that your assistant can answer straight away using your guidance. Examples include confirming a meeting time, sending a link to your booking page, answering common questions about your services or acknowledging a document.

### 2. Needs you

Messages that require your decision, expertise or personal touch. Your assistant moves these to a dedicated folder or label, and adds a short summary at the top so you can deal with each one quickly.

### 3. Waiting on someone

Emails where the next step is someone else's. Your assistant tracks these and follows up if there is no reply after an agreed time, often two or three working days.

### 4. For reference

Receipts, confirmations, contracts and anything you may need later. These are filed in a clear folder structure, so they are easy to find but not cluttering your inbox.

### 5. Not needed

Newsletters you never read, notifications that do not need action and promotional emails. These are archived, and your assistant unsubscribes you from anything that keeps arriving without value.

## Set up the labels or folders

Create five labels or folders with clear names, for example:

- 1 Reply now
- 2 Needs you
- 3 Waiting
- 4 Reference
- 5 Archive

Numbering them keeps them in order at the top of your folder list. Your assistant works through new messages several times a day and moves each one to the right place.

## Write a short reply guide

The "Reply now" category only works if your assistant knows how you like to respond. Spend an hour writing a simple guide that covers:

- How you sign off and which name to use
- Your tone, for example warm and professional
- Answers to your most common questions
- Links you share often, such as your booking page, price list or portfolio
- Topics your assistant should never answer, such as pricing exceptions or complaints

Over time, your assistant can turn the replies you approve into saved templates, so common answers stay consistent and quick.

## Agree when you will check "Needs you"

The biggest change for most business owners is checking email at set times rather than all day. Choose two or three times a day to go through your "Needs you" folder, for example at 10am and 3pm.

Because your assistant has already summarised each message and handled everything else, these sessions are short. Many people find that their email time drops from hours to minutes once the system is running.

## Use a daily summary

At the end of each day, your assistant sends you a short summary:

- How many emails came in
- What was answered on your behalf
- What is waiting for your decision
- Any follow-ups that are overdue

This keeps you informed without needing to read every message, and it builds trust in the system quickly.

## Protect your privacy and security

Giving someone access to your inbox is a big step, and it should be done carefully.

- Share access through delegated access or a password manager, not by sending your password
- Turn on two-step verification
- Agree in writing which folders or topics are private
- Review access regularly and remove it straight away if the arrangement ends

Many email providers allow you to give someone access to your mailbox without sharing your password at all. It is worth using that option where you can.

## What to expect in the first month

In the first week, your assistant will ask a lot of questions. That is a good sign. Each answer becomes part of the guide.

By the second or third week, most routine emails are handled without you, and your "Needs you" folder becomes small and focused.

By the end of the first month, the system usually runs smoothly, and you can start adding related tasks such as calendar management, lead follow-up and client reminders.

## Your next step

Set up the five folders this week and try sorting your own inbox with them for a few days. You will quickly see how many emails you do not need to handle personally.
