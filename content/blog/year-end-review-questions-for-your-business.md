The end of the year is a natural moment to pause. Before you set new goals, it is worth looking back honestly at the year you have just had. What worked? What drained you? What would you do differently?

A year-end review does not need to take days. With a cup of tea and a couple of quiet hours, these ten questions will give you a clear picture of your business and a strong starting point for the year ahead.

Write your answers down. Thinking is useful, but writing makes things clearer and gives you something to look back on next year.

## 1. What am I most proud of this year?

Start with the wins. Business owners are often quick to notice what went wrong and slow to celebrate what went right. List your achievements, big and small: new clients, finished projects, skills you learned, challenges you got through.

## 2. Which clients or projects did I enjoy most, and why?

Think about the work that energised you. What did those clients or projects have in common? Perhaps it was the type of work, the industry, the size of the project or the way the client communicated. These patterns show you what kind of business you want more of.

## 3. Which clients or projects drained me, and why?

Now look at the opposite. Which work felt heavy? Were there warning signs at the start? Did you take it on for the wrong reasons? Understanding this helps you choose clients more carefully and set clearer boundaries next year.

## 4. Where did my new clients come from?

List your new clients and how each one found you. Referrals, social media, your website, networking, past clients returning? This tells you where to focus your marketing effort next year, and what you can stop doing.

## 5. Where did my time actually go?

Look back through your calendar for a few typical weeks. How much time went on client work, and how much on admin, email, scheduling and chasing things? For many business owners, this question is the most revealing. The admin often takes far more time than expected.

## 6. What did I keep putting off?

Every business has tasks that roll over from week to week. Updating the website, organising files, following up with old leads, setting up a proper system for invoices. Write them down. These are often perfect tasks to delegate or to schedule properly in the new year.

## 7. Which systems worked, and which broke?

Think about how your business runs. Which processes made life easier? Which ones caused mistakes, delays or stress? Perhaps your booking process worked smoothly but your inbox regularly got out of control. Note what to keep and what to fix.

## 8. How did my finances look?

You do not need to be an accountant to answer this. Look at the basics:

- Did income grow, stay steady or drop?
- Which services brought in the most income?
- Which costs were worth it, and which were not?
- Were there months when cash flow was tight?

If you work with a bookkeeper or accountant, this is a good time to ask them for a simple summary.

## 9. How did I feel this year?

This question is easy to skip, but it matters. Were you energised or exhausted? Did you take breaks? Did you have time for family, friends and rest? A business that succeeds on paper but leaves you burnt out is not sustainable.

## 10. What would make next year better?

Now bring it together. Based on everything above, write down:

- Three things to keep doing
- Three things to stop doing
- Three things to start doing

Keep it focused. Three changes you actually make are worth far more than a long list you forget by February.

## Turn your answers into a plan

Once you have your answers, pick the most important change and make a plan for the first quarter. For example, if question five showed that admin is taking up much of your week, your first goal might be to hand off your inbox and scheduling by the end of January.

Put the first step in your calendar. A plan without a date is a wish.

## Make it a habit

A year-end review is even more useful when you can compare it with the year before. Keep your answers somewhere safe, and consider doing a shorter version every three months. Small, regular reviews help you notice problems early and keep your plans on track.

## Your next step

Find two quiet hours before the new year begins and work through the questions. Be honest with yourself. The answers are only for you.
