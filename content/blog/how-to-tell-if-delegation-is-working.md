Handing off tasks is the first step. The next question is whether it is making a real difference. Are you getting time back? Is the work being done well? Is your business running more smoothly, or have you simply moved the stress somewhere else?

Without a way to measure it, it is easy to rely on a vague feeling. Some weeks you feel relieved, some weeks you feel like you are still doing everything. Here is how to tell, more clearly, whether delegation is working for you.

## Sign 1: You are getting real hours back

The most obvious measure is time. Compare a typical week now with a typical week before you delegated. Are you spending less time on email, scheduling, admin and follow-ups?

A simple way to check is to track your time for one week, the same way you may have done before you started delegating. Look at how many hours go to tasks only you can do, compared with before.

If you are not getting time back, ask why. Sometimes it is because you are still checking every piece of work. Sometimes new tasks have filled the space. Both can be fixed.

## Sign 2: You are not checking everything twice

In the early weeks, reviewing your assistant's work is normal and useful. After a month or two, though, you should be checking far less. If you still feel you need to review everything, something is not working.

It might be that the instructions need improving, the tasks are too complex for the current stage or there is a trust issue that needs an honest conversation. Identify which it is and address it directly.

## Sign 3: Things are not falling through the cracks

Good delegation should make your business more reliable, not less. Look for signs such as:

- Inquiries are answered quickly and consistently
- Follow-ups happen on time
- Invoices go out when they should
- Your calendar is organised and up to date
- Clients are not chasing you for replies

If problems are increasing, look at whether the processes are clear and whether your assistant has everything they need.

## Sign 4: You know what is happening without asking

When delegation works, you feel informed without having to chase. A short daily or weekly update from your assistant should tell you what has been done, what is pending and what needs your input.

If you regularly find yourself asking "did that get done?", set up a simple reporting routine. It will save you both time and worry.

## Sign 5: You are spending time on higher-value work

Getting time back only matters if you use it well. Look at what you are doing with the hours you have freed up. Are you spending more time with clients, creating, planning, selling or resting? Or has the time quietly filled with other low-value tasks?

Be intentional. Decide in advance what you will do with the time you get back, and protect it in your calendar.

## Sign 6: You feel less stressed

This one is harder to measure, but it matters. Do you feel less overwhelmed than before? Can you take an afternoon off without worrying? Do you sleep better on Sunday nights?

If delegation is working, the answer should increasingly be yes. If you feel more stressed, it is worth looking at why. Often it comes down to unclear expectations or a lack of trust, both of which can be fixed.

## What to do if it is not working

If several of these signs are missing, do not give up on delegation. Usually the problem is in the setup, not the idea. Common fixes include:

### Clarify the instructions

Many issues come from unclear expectations. Review the guides for each task. Are the steps clear? Is it obvious what a good result looks like? Update them together with your assistant.

### Reduce the scope temporarily

If too much was handed off too quickly, step back. Focus on two or three tasks until they run smoothly, then add more.

### Have an honest conversation

Talk openly with your assistant about what is and is not working. Ask what would help them. Often they have noticed the same issues and have useful ideas.

### Check the fit

Occasionally the person is not the right match for the work. That is not a failure, it happens. If you work with a managed agency, they can help address it or arrange a better match. If you hired independently, it may be time to look again.

## A simple monthly check

Once a month, ask yourself these questions:

- How many hours did delegation save me this month?
- How much time did I spend reviewing work?
- Did anything fall through the cracks?
- Do I know what my assistant is working on?
- What did I do with the time I got back?
- How do I feel compared with last month?

Write the answers down. Over a few months, you will see clear trends.

## Your next step

Do the monthly check at the end of this month, even if you only started delegating recently. It will show you what to adjust as you head into the new year.
