"Virtual assistant" is one of those job titles that means different things to different people. Some picture someone who only answers email. Others imagine a personal assistant who books restaurants. In reality, a good virtual assistant can handle a wide range of business tasks, and the right mix depends on what your business needs.

If you have been wondering whether a virtual assistant could help you, it helps to see what their week can look like. Here is an example of a typical week supporting a busy service business owner.

## Every morning: inbox and priorities

Most days start with the inbox. A virtual assistant works through new emails, replies to routine messages using your preferred answers and sorts everything else into clear categories. Anything that needs your decision is flagged with a short summary, so you can deal with it in minutes rather than hours.

They also check your calendar for the day, confirm any meetings and prepare what you need, such as links, notes or documents.

## Throughout the day: inquiries and follow-ups

New inquiries can arrive at any time: through your website, by email, on social media or through messaging apps. A virtual assistant monitors these channels, sends a prompt and personal first reply and invites potential clients to book a discovery call.

They also keep track of people who have not replied yet and send polite follow-ups at the right time. This alone can make a noticeable difference to how many inquiries turn into clients.

## Monday: planning the week

At the start of the week, an assistant might:

- Review your calendar and flag clashes or overloaded days
- Prepare a list of the week's priorities and deadlines
- Check which invoices are due or overdue
- Update your CRM with the latest status of each lead

## Tuesday: client support

On a typical Tuesday, an assistant might:

- Send onboarding emails and documents to a new client
- Schedule a kickoff call
- Prepare a project folder with everything the client needs
- Answer client questions that do not need your input

## Wednesday: content and social media

Midweek is often a good time for marketing tasks:

- Scheduling the week's social media posts
- Replying to comments and messages
- Drafting a newsletter or blog post from your notes
- Creating simple graphics using your brand templates

## Thursday: admin and finance

Admin rarely disappears on its own. An assistant can:

- Send invoices and payment reminders
- Track expenses and file receipts
- Update spreadsheets and records
- Research suppliers, tools or travel options

## Friday: wrapping up and reporting

At the end of the week, your assistant can:

- Send a weekly summary of what was done
- Highlight anything waiting for your input
- Tidy files and update notes
- Prepare a plan for the following week

You start the weekend knowing exactly where things stand.

## Tasks a virtual assistant often handles

Beyond the routine above, virtual assistants commonly help with:

- **Email and calendar management**
- **Lead follow-up and appointment setting**
- **Customer service** by email, chat or messaging apps
- **Social media scheduling and engagement**
- **CRM updates and simple automations**
- **Online store support**, such as order updates and product listings
- **Document formatting**, proposals and presentations
- **Research and data entry**

## What a virtual assistant does not usually do

It is also helpful to know the limits. A general virtual assistant is usually not a replacement for specialist professionals such as accountants, lawyers or senior strategists. They can support those people, for example by organising documents or scheduling meetings, but they do not take on regulated or specialist responsibilities.

## How much time could it save you?

That depends on your week. The best way to find out is to keep a list of every task you do for one week. Then mark each task that someone else could do with clear guidance. Many business owners are surprised by how much of their week falls into that group.

## Getting started

You do not need to hand over everything at once. Most people start with two or three tasks, usually inbox management, calendar scheduling and lead follow-up, and add more as trust grows.
