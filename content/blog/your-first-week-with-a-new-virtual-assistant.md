You have found the right assistant. Now what? The first week is where many working relationships are made or broken. If it goes well, your assistant understands your business, knows what to do and starts saving you time quickly. If it is rushed or unclear, you can spend weeks fixing misunderstandings.

A good onboarding does not need to be complicated. It needs to be clear, organised and paced. Here is a day-by-day checklist for the first week.

## Before day one: prepare the basics

A little preparation before your assistant starts makes the first day much smoother.

- **Sign the paperwork.** Make sure the agreement and confidentiality terms are signed.
- **Set up access.** Create accounts or delegated access for the tools they will need in the first week only. Use a password manager to share any logins.
- **Choose the first tasks.** Pick two or three tasks to start with. Not ten.
- **Gather your guides.** Collect any notes, templates or screen recordings for those tasks.
- **Agree how you will communicate.** Decide on one main channel for messages and how quickly you expect replies.

## Day 1: Welcome and context

The first day is about understanding your business, not doing tasks.

- Have a video call of about an hour
- Explain what your business does, who your clients are and what makes you different
- Share your goals for the next few months
- Walk through the tools they will use
- Explain how you like to communicate, including your working hours and when you prefer not to be disturbed
- Answer their questions

Send a short written summary of the call afterwards. It becomes a reference your assistant can return to.

## Day 2: Show the first task

Pick the first task and show how you do it.

- Do the task while sharing your screen, explaining each step
- Record it so they can watch it again
- Point out what a good result looks like and any common mistakes
- Ask them to write the steps down in their own words

Having your assistant write the guide is a great way to check their understanding. It also means the guide is written in a way that makes sense to the person using it.

## Day 3: They try, you review

Now your assistant does the task while you are available to answer questions.

- Let them complete the task on their own
- Review the result together
- Give specific, kind feedback: what worked and what to change
- Update the written guide with anything new

Expect small mistakes at this stage. They are part of learning, and every correction improves the guide.

## Day 4: Add the second task

Repeat the same pattern for the second task: show it, record it and let them try. Meanwhile, they keep doing the first task on their own, with a lighter review from you.

## Day 5: Review the week

At the end of the week, have a short call to look back.

- What went well?
- What was confusing?
- Which tools or access caused problems?
- Is the communication working for both of you?
- What should be added next week?

Agree on the plan for week two. Usually this means continuing the first tasks, adding one or two more and starting regular check-ins.

## Set up a simple rhythm

Beyond the first week, a regular rhythm keeps things running smoothly.

- **Daily:** a short end-of-day update from your assistant covering what was done, what is pending and any questions
- **Weekly:** a fifteen to thirty minute check-in to review priorities and add new tasks
- **Monthly:** a slightly longer review of how things are going overall

## Tips for a smooth start

### Be clear about priorities

When everything feels urgent, nothing is. Tell your assistant which tasks matter most, so they know what to focus on if time is short.

### Explain the why, not just the how

When your assistant understands why a task matters, they can make better decisions when something unexpected comes up.

### Give feedback early

Small issues are much easier to fix in the first week than after a month. Be honest and specific, and invite your assistant to give you feedback too.

### Resist the urge to take it back

When a task is not done exactly your way, it is tempting to just do it yourself. That keeps you stuck. Explain what to change and let them try again.

## A one-page checklist

- Agreement and confidentiality signed
- Access set up through a password manager
- First two or three tasks chosen
- Guides or recordings ready
- Communication channel agreed
- Day 1 welcome call and written summary
- Days 2 to 4: show, try, review
- Day 5 review call and plan for week two
- Daily updates and weekly check-ins in place

## How it works with a managed agency

When you work with a managed agency, much of this is handled for you. The agency helps you choose the first tasks, sets up secure access and supports your assistant as they learn your business. You still play an important part, especially in explaining your business and giving feedback, but you are not doing it alone.
