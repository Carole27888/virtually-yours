Most business owners who decide to get help hit the same wall straight away. They know they are doing too much, but when they sit down to hand something off, they freeze. Everything feels important. Everything feels like it needs them.

The good news is that choosing what to delegate first is not a gut-feel decision. There is a simple process that works for almost any business, and it takes less than an hour.

## Start with a week of honest notes

Before you decide anything, find out where your time actually goes. For one normal working week, keep a running list of every task you do. Write it down as you go, not at the end of the day, because memory is kind to us and leaves out the small jobs that add up.

Your list will probably include things like:

- Answering new inquiries
- Replying to client emails
- Scheduling and rescheduling calls
- Updating your CRM or spreadsheet
- Sending invoices and chasing payments
- Posting on social media
- Booking travel
- Formatting documents and proposals

Do not judge the list while you write it. Just capture it. By Friday you will have a clear picture of your week, and it is usually a surprise.

## Sort every task into three groups

Now go through the list and put each task into one of three groups.

### 1. Only you can do it

These are the tasks that depend on your expertise, your relationships or your decisions. Leading a client strategy session, setting prices, creating your signature work and having difficult conversations with key clients all belong here. Keep these.

### 2. Someone else could do it with guidance

These tasks follow a pattern. Someone with good judgement could do them well once they understand how you like things done. Drafting replies to common emails, preparing proposals from a template and managing your calendar often fit here. These are excellent candidates for delegation, with a little training at the start.

### 3. Anyone organised could do it

These are repeatable, clearly defined tasks with an obvious right answer. Data entry, scheduling posts, filing documents, sending reminders and updating records are classic examples. These should go first.

## Choose your first three tasks

From groups two and three, pick three tasks to hand off first. Use these questions to choose:

1. **Which tasks happen most often?** Daily and weekly tasks free up far more time than monthly ones.
2. **Which tasks drain your energy?** Some jobs take ten minutes but leave you dreading them all morning. Those are worth handing off even if they are small.
3. **Which tasks are slowing your business down?** If inquiries wait days for a reply, or invoices go out late, delegating those tasks helps your income as well as your schedule.
4. **Which tasks are easiest to explain?** Your first handoffs should be simple, so you and your assistant can build trust quickly.

The sweet spot is a task that happens often, drains you and is easy to explain. Inbox triage, calendar management and lead follow-up tick all three boxes for most business owners.

## Why not delegate everything at once?

It is tempting, once you feel the relief of handing something off, to give away a long list in week one. Resist that urge. Starting with a few tasks lets you see how your assistant works, refine your instructions and fix any gaps before they matter. It also builds your own confidence in delegating, which is a skill like any other.

Once the first three tasks are running smoothly, usually within two to four weeks, add the next few. Over a couple of months you can move most of groups two and three off your plate without any of the chaos.

## Write down how you want things done

For each task you hand off, write a short guide. It does not need to be formal. Cover:

- What the task is and why it matters
- How often it needs doing
- Which tools and logins are needed
- The steps, in order
- What a good result looks like

A short screen recording of you doing the task once is often more useful than a page of text. Your assistant can watch it, ask questions and then write the guide themselves, which is a great way to check that they have understood.

## Common worries, answered

**"It will take longer to explain than to do it myself."** For the first week, maybe. After that, you get that time back every single week, for as long as the task exists.

**"Nobody will do it the way I do."** Possibly true, and often fine. Decide which parts of the task truly need to be done your way, and be clear about those. For the rest, a different approach that gets the same result is not a problem.

**"I cannot give someone access to my accounts."** This is a real concern, and a fair one. A good assistant or agency will use a password manager, confidentiality agreements and limited access so your information stays protected. We will cover this in more detail in a later post.

## What you keep matters as much as what you hand off

Delegation is not about doing less work. It is about doing more of the right work. When the admin, follow-ups and scheduling are handled, you get your best hours back for the things that only you can do: serving clients, building relationships and growing your business.
