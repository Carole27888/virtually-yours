The first weeks of January have a way of filling up fast. Clients return from their breaks, new inquiries arrive, plans need making and the admin that waited over the holidays suddenly needs attention. If you do it all yourself, the year can start in a rush that lasts until spring.

The last few days of December are a good time to decide what you will not be doing yourself next year. Here are the tasks most worth handing off before January begins, and how to get them ready.

## 1. Your inbox

Email is usually the biggest daily drain on a business owner's time. Having someone sort, answer and summarise your inbox can give you back a large part of every day.

**To get it ready:** write a short reply guide with your tone, common answers and topics that need you. Set up folders or labels for sorting.

## 2. Your calendar and scheduling

Booking calls, rescheduling, sending reminders and preparing meeting notes all add up. An assistant can manage your calendar so your week is planned around your priorities.

**To get it ready:** write down your preferred working hours, the times you want to keep free and how long you need between meetings. Share your booking link.

## 3. Lead follow-up

New year, new inquiries. Many potential clients reach out in January as they make plans for the year. Fast replies and consistent follow-up can make a real difference to how many become clients.

**To get it ready:** save templates for your first reply and follow-ups, and decide on your follow-up timing. Make sure your leads are in one place, such as a simple CRM.

## 4. Social media posting

January is a good time to be visible, while many businesses are still quiet. Handing off planning, scheduling and replying keeps your accounts active without you thinking about it every day.

**To get it ready:** choose your main platforms and three or four content themes. Gather any photos or brand materials your assistant will need.

## 5. Invoicing and payment reminders

Getting paid on time matters, especially at the start of the year. Sending invoices, tracking payments and sending polite reminders is a straightforward task to delegate.

**To get it ready:** decide who can create invoices and in which tool, and write a short, friendly reminder template.

## 6. Client onboarding

If you take on new clients in January, a smooth onboarding process makes a great first impression. Welcome emails, questionnaires, contracts and kickoff scheduling can all be handled by an assistant.

**To get it ready:** list the steps you take with every new client and save your welcome email and documents as templates.

## 7. Research and preparation

Preparing for meetings, researching suppliers, comparing tools or gathering information for a project can take hours. An assistant can do the groundwork and give you a summary.

**To get it ready:** be clear about what you need, the format you prefer and when you need it.

## 8. Your CRM and records

Starting the year with clean, up-to-date records makes everything easier. An assistant can tidy your CRM, update contact details and set reminders for the year ahead.

**To get it ready:** give access to your CRM or spreadsheet and agree how you want it organised.

## Do not hand off everything at once

Looking at this list, it might be tempting to hand all of it over on the first day of January. A better approach is to choose two or three tasks to start, get them running smoothly and add more over the following weeks. You will build trust faster and avoid the confusion of too many changes at once.

A good starting combination for many business owners is inbox management, calendar scheduling and lead follow-up. Together, they often free up the most time and have a direct effect on income.

## Keep what matters most

Delegation is not about stepping away from your business. It is about spending your time where it counts. Keep the client relationships, the creative work, the strategy and the decisions. Hand off the rest.

## A quick pre-January checklist

- Choose two or three tasks to hand off first
- Write a short guide for each
- Save the templates you use most
- Set up secure access through a password manager
- Agree on daily updates and weekly check-ins
- Block focused time in your January calendar for the work only you can do
