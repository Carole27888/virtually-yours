Once you decide you need help, the next question is how to get it. You could hire a freelancer for specific projects, bring on an independent virtual assistant or work with a managed agency that provides an assistant and supports them.

Each option can work well. The right choice depends on what you need, how much involvement you want and how important continuity is to your business. Here is an honest look at each one.

## Option 1: A freelancer for specific projects

Freelancers are independent professionals who usually focus on a particular skill, such as design, copywriting, bookkeeping or web development. You hire them for a defined project or a set number of hours.

**Works well when:**

- You need a specific skill for a clear, one-off project
- The work has a defined start and end
- You are comfortable briefing and managing the project yourself

**Things to consider:**

- Freelancers often juggle many clients, so availability can vary
- They usually focus on one skill, so you may need several freelancers for different tasks
- Ongoing, day-to-day support is often not what they are set up for

## Option 2: An independent virtual assistant

An independent virtual assistant works for themselves and supports a handful of clients on an ongoing basis. They often help with email, scheduling, admin, social media and customer service.

**Works well when:**

- You want ongoing support with a variety of tasks
- You are happy to find, vet, train and manage the person yourself
- You have the time to build systems and give regular feedback

**Things to consider:**

- You are responsible for finding and checking the right person
- If your assistant is ill, goes on holiday or decides to move on, your support stops until you find someone else
- Quality depends entirely on one person, with no one else checking the work
- You will need to handle training, processes and security yourself

## Option 3: A managed agency

A managed agency matches you with a dedicated assistant, but the agency also sits behind them. It typically handles recruitment, vetting, training, quality checks and cover if your assistant is unavailable.

**Works well when:**

- You want ongoing support without managing the person day to day
- Continuity matters, and you cannot afford gaps when someone is away
- You want help building the systems, not just doing the tasks
- You would value having more than one skill available, such as social media or CRM setup, without hiring separately

**Things to consider:**

- It usually costs more than an independent assistant, because you are paying for the support around them
- You may have a little less say in the exact working arrangement, as the agency will have its own processes

## Questions to help you decide

Ask yourself these questions honestly.

1. **How much time do I have to manage someone?** If the answer is very little, a managed option will probably suit you better.
2. **What happens if my help disappears for two weeks?** If that would cause serious problems, continuity should be a priority.
3. **Do I need one skill or several?** For one specialised project, a freelancer may be best. For a range of ongoing tasks, an assistant makes more sense.
4. **Do I already have systems and processes?** If you do, an independent assistant can step into them. If you do not, you may want help creating them.
5. **How sensitive is the information involved?** Make sure whoever you work with has clear practices around confidentiality and access.

## What to look for, whichever option you choose

- **Clear communication.** You should know how and when you will hear from them.
- **References or a trial period.** A short trial lets you test the working relationship before committing.
- **Security practices.** Ask how they handle passwords, access and confidential information.
- **A written agreement.** Scope, hours, pricing, notice periods and confidentiality should all be in writing.
- **Time zone fit.** Make sure their working hours overlap with yours where it matters.
