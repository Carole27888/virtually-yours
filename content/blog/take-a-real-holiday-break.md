Every year, many business owners promise themselves a proper break over the holidays. And every year, many of them spend it half present, checking their phone at the dinner table, replying to emails in the evenings and worrying about what they might be missing.

Rest is not a luxury. You make better decisions, have more ideas and serve clients better when you have had a real break. Here is how to take one this year, without your business grinding to a halt.

## Decide what "off" means for you

Not everyone can, or wants to, disconnect completely. The important thing is to decide in advance what your break looks like, rather than letting it happen by accident. Options include:

- **Fully off:** no email, no messages, no work at all
- **Check once a day:** fifteen minutes each morning, then put the phone away
- **Emergencies only:** someone else monitors everything and contacts you only if something truly urgent happens

Choose one and commit to it. Write down what counts as an emergency so you are not deciding in the moment.

## Tell clients early

Let clients know your holiday hours well before your break. Include:

- The dates you will be away
- How quickly they can expect replies during that time
- Who to contact for anything urgent
- When normal service resumes

Most clients appreciate the notice, and many will be taking time off themselves. Clear communication prevents surprises and awkward messages on your days off.

## Finish or pause your open work

Before your break, go through your active projects. For each one, either finish it, reach a natural stopping point or agree with the client on when it will continue. Write a short note for each project with the next step, so you can pick it up easily when you are back.

## Set up your out-of-office message

A good out-of-office message is short and helpful. For example:

> Thank you for your email. Our office is closed from 24 December to 2 January. We will reply to your message when we return. For anything urgent, please contact our team at the address below.

If you have someone covering while you are away, include their contact details. If not, be clear about when you will reply.

## Keep your marketing running

You do not have to go silent just because you are resting. Schedule a few social media posts and, if you send one, your newsletter before you leave. Holiday greetings, a look back at the year and helpful tips for the new year all work well.

## Have someone watch the inbox

This is the biggest difference between a break that feels restful and one that does not. When someone you trust is monitoring your email and messages, you can stop checking them yourself.

A virtual assistant can:

- Reply to routine messages using your saved answers
- Send your holiday hours to new inquiries
- Book discovery calls for after your return
- Flag only genuine emergencies, using the rules you agreed
- Send you a short summary on your first day back

You return to an organised inbox, not a mountain of unread messages.

## Remove temptation

Even with everything in place, habit can pull you back. A few practical steps help:

- Remove work email from your phone for the break, or turn off its notifications
- Log out of work apps on personal devices
- Set your phone to a focus or do not disturb mode
- Ask family or friends to call you out if they see you working

## Plan your first day back

The first day back can undo all the benefits of your break if it starts in chaos. Before you leave, block out your first morning in your calendar for catching up. Avoid booking calls on that day if you can. When you return, start with your assistant's summary, then your priority list.

## A holiday break checklist

- Decide what "off" means for you
- Tell clients your holiday hours
- Finish or pause open projects, with written next steps
- Set an out-of-office message
- Schedule social media posts
- Arrange inbox and message cover
- Agree what counts as an emergency
- Turn off work notifications
- Block your first morning back for catching up

## Rest is good for business

It can feel strange to think of rest as part of running a business, but it is. The ideas, energy and patience you bring back after a real break are worth far more than the few emails you might have answered while away.

## Your next step

Go through the checklist today and tick off what you can. Even two or three items will make your break noticeably calmer.
