Most business owners know they should post on social media regularly. Most also know the feeling of opening the app, staring at a blank caption box and deciding to do it tomorrow. Tomorrow turns into next week, and suddenly your last post was a month ago.

The problem is rarely a lack of ideas. It is trying to think of what to post on the same day you need to post it. The answer is to plan in batches. Here is how to plan a full month of content in a single afternoon.

## Before you start: know what you are aiming for

Decide two things first:

- **Where you will post.** Choose one or two platforms where your clients actually spend time. It is better to do one platform well than four badly.
- **How often you will post.** Three times a week is a realistic, sustainable target for most small businesses. That is about twelve or thirteen posts a month.

## Step 1: Choose your content pillars

Content pillars are the main themes you post about. They keep your content focused and make it much easier to come up with ideas. Pick three or four. For a service business, these often work well:

1. **Helpful tips:** practical advice related to your expertise
2. **Behind the scenes:** how you work, your process and your values
3. **Results and proof:** examples of your work, with permission, or answers to common questions
4. **Offers and invitations:** what you offer and how to work with you

Aim for a mix. If every post is an offer, people stop listening. If you never mention what you offer, people may not know they can hire you.

## Step 2: Brainstorm ideas for each pillar

Set a timer for twenty minutes and write down as many ideas as you can for each pillar. Do not judge them yet. Good sources of ideas include:

- Questions clients ask you often
- Mistakes you see people make in your field
- Things you wish clients knew before working with you
- Your favourite tools and how you use them
- Seasonal moments, such as the new year, holidays or busy periods in your industry

Once the timer stops, pick the best ideas, around three or four per pillar.

## Step 3: Map ideas onto a calendar

Open a simple calendar, a spreadsheet or a planning tool, and assign each idea to a date. Rotate your pillars so the mix stays balanced across each week. Add any key dates, such as launches, events or holidays, and plan relevant posts around them.

## Step 4: Write all the captions in one go

Now write every caption, one after another. Writing in a batch is faster because you stay in the same frame of mind. A simple structure for each caption:

1. **A hook:** a first line that makes people want to read on
2. **The value:** the tip, story or information
3. **A call to action:** what you want them to do next, such as save the post, comment or book a call

Keep captions clear and conversational. Write the way you would speak to a client.

## Step 5: Create the visuals

Use a design tool with templates in your brand colours and fonts, so every graphic looks consistent and you are not starting from scratch each time. Simple text graphics, photos of your work and short videos all work well. Create everything for the month in the same session.

## Step 6: Schedule everything

Most platforms and many third-party tools let you schedule posts in advance. Load all of your posts with their captions and visuals, set the dates and times and check that everything looks right.

Once your posts are scheduled, your social media runs on its own for the month. You only need to check in to reply to comments and messages.

## Step 7: Reply and engage

Scheduling handles posting, but social media is still social. Set aside a few minutes each day, or delegate this, to:

- Reply to comments on your posts
- Answer direct messages
- Engage with posts from clients and people in your industry

This is often where real conversations and new inquiries start.

## A simple afternoon plan

- **30 minutes:** choose pillars and brainstorm ideas
- **20 minutes:** map ideas onto the calendar
- **90 minutes:** write captions
- **60 minutes:** create visuals
- **30 minutes:** schedule everything

That is under four hours for a full month of consistent posting.

## Review at the end of the month

Before planning the next month, take a quick look at how your posts performed. Which ones got the most saves, comments or messages? Do more of what worked and adjust what did not.

## Your next step

Block out an afternoon this week and try the process. Even if you only plan two weeks the first time, you will feel the difference immediately.
