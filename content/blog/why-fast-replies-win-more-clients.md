Think about the last time you needed a service. You probably contacted a few businesses, maybe filled in a couple of forms and sent an email or two. Who did you end up talking to first? Very often, it was the business that replied first.

For service businesses, response time is one of the most underrated parts of sales. Your work might be excellent, your prices fair and your reviews glowing. But if a potential client waits two days for a reply, there is a good chance they have already booked a call with someone else.

## Why speed matters so much

When someone reaches out to you, they are at their most interested. They have a problem, they have decided to do something about it and they have chosen you as one of their options. That moment does not last long.

A quick reply does three things:

1. **It catches them while they are still motivated.** The longer they wait, the more likely they are to get busy, change their mind or move on.
2. **It shows them how you work.** A fast, clear response suggests you are organised and reliable. A slow one raises doubts, even if the delay was for a good reason.
3. **It puts you first in line.** When people contact several businesses, the first useful reply often sets the standard the others are measured against.

## Why replies get delayed

Most slow replies are not caused by a lack of care. They happen because the business owner is busy doing the actual work. You might be with a client, on a shoot, in a meeting or travelling. By the time you see the message, it is evening and you are tired.

Common causes include:

- Inquiries arriving in several places: email, website form, Instagram, WhatsApp
- No one checking messages while you are busy with clients
- Not having standard answers ready, so each reply takes thought
- Waiting until you have time to write the perfect response

## How to reply faster without working longer hours

### Bring everything into one place

If inquiries arrive in several places, it is easy to miss some. Wherever possible, direct people to one main channel, such as a contact form that sends to your email or a booking link. For channels you cannot avoid, make sure someone checks each one at set times every day.

### Prepare your answers in advance

Most inquiries ask similar questions: what you offer, how much it costs, whether you are available and what happens next. Write a clear answer to each one and save them as templates. A reply that once took twenty minutes can then take two.

### Reply with a next step, not just information

The best first reply moves the conversation forward. Instead of only answering the question, invite the person to take the next step: book a call, choose a date or fill in a short questionnaire. A booking link in your reply removes the back-and-forth of finding a time.

### Set a response time and stick to it

Decide on a standard, for example replying to every inquiry within a few business hours. Write it down. Once you have a clear target, it becomes much easier to notice when things are slipping.

### Use an automatic acknowledgement, carefully

An automatic reply that confirms you have received the message and says when you will respond can help. It reassures the person that they have not been ignored. But it is not a substitute for a real reply. Keep it short and make sure a personal response follows soon after.

### Have someone else handle first replies

This is where delegation makes the biggest difference. A trained assistant can monitor your inquiries throughout the day, send a personal first reply using your templates and book discovery calls straight into your calendar. You step in when the conversation needs your expertise, not before.

## Follow-up matters too

A fast first reply is only half the story. Many potential clients do not book straight away. They are comparing options, waiting for approval or simply busy.

A polite follow-up a few days later, and another a week after that, often brings the conversation back to life. Most businesses never send these follow-ups, so doing it consistently can set you apart. A simple CRM or even a well-organised spreadsheet can remind you, or your assistant, when each follow-up is due.

## A simple response plan

- One main place for inquiries, with every channel checked daily
- Saved answers to your most common questions
- A booking link in every first reply
- A clear response time target
- A short automatic acknowledgement
- Follow-ups after three days and again after a week
- Someone responsible for all of the above, even when you are busy

## Your next step

Look at your last ten inquiries. How long did each one wait for a reply? How many received a follow-up? The answers will show you where the quickest wins are.
