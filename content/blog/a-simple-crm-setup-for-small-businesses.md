If your leads live in your inbox, your notes app, a spreadsheet and your memory, some of them are slipping through the cracks. Not because you do not care, but because there is no single place that tells you who you are talking to and what needs to happen next.

That is what a CRM is for. CRM stands for customer relationship management, and while the name sounds corporate, the idea is simple: one place that holds every lead and client, and reminds you what to do next.

Here is a simple setup that works for most small service businesses.

## Choose a tool you will actually use

There are many CRM tools available, from free options to powerful paid platforms. For a small business, the best CRM is the one you and your team will keep up to date. Look for:

- An easy, visual way to see all your leads at once
- Simple reminders for follow-ups
- A way to connect your contact form or email
- A free plan or trial so you can test it first

If you are not ready for a dedicated tool, a well-organised spreadsheet can work at the start. The principles below apply either way.

## Set up your pipeline stages

A pipeline is the path a lead takes from first contact to becoming a client. Keep it short. Five or six stages are plenty for most businesses:

1. **New inquiry:** someone has contacted you
2. **Contacted:** you have replied
3. **Call booked:** a discovery call or consultation is scheduled
4. **Proposal sent:** you have sent pricing or a proposal
5. **Won:** they have become a client
6. **Lost:** they have decided not to go ahead, for now

Each lead sits in one stage at a time and moves along as the conversation progresses. At a glance, you can see how many people are at each step.

## Decide what to record for each lead

Keep the fields simple. You can always add more later. A good starting point is:

- Name and business name
- Email and phone number
- Where they found you
- What they are interested in
- Estimated value
- Next step and its due date
- Notes from conversations

The most important field is **next step and due date**. A lead without a next step is a lead that will be forgotten.

## Connect your inquiry sources

The less copying and pasting, the better. Most CRMs can connect to your website contact form so new inquiries appear automatically. Some can also connect to your email or booking tool.

If a connection is not possible, set a simple rule: every new inquiry is added to the CRM on the same day, by you or your assistant.

## Create follow-up reminders

The real value of a CRM is not storing information. It is making sure nobody falls through the cracks. For each stage, decide what follow-up should happen and when. For example:

- **Contacted, no reply:** follow up after three days
- **Proposal sent, no decision:** check in after five days
- **Lost:** check in again in three months

Your CRM can create these reminders automatically, or you can set them by hand with each update.

## Save your common emails as templates

Most CRMs let you save email templates. Write templates for your first reply, follow-ups, call confirmations and proposal check-ins. Personalise each one before sending, but start from the template so you never have to write from scratch.

## Keep it up to date with a weekly review

A CRM is only useful if it reflects reality. Set aside fifteen minutes each week to:

- Move leads to the right stage
- Check that every lead has a next step and date
- Close out any leads that have gone quiet for too long
- Look at where new leads came from

This short review also gives you a clear picture of your sales, which is useful for planning.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Too many fields.** If updating a record takes five minutes, it will not happen. Keep it lean.
- **Too many stages.** Each extra stage is another decision. Start simple.
- **No owner.** Someone must be responsible for keeping it updated, whether that is you or your assistant.
- **Treating it as a contact list.** The value is in the next steps, not the names.

## Let someone else maintain it

Keeping a CRM updated is a perfect task to delegate. A virtual assistant can add new inquiries, log conversations, send follow-ups from your templates and give you a short weekly summary of your pipeline. You see the big picture and step in for the conversations that need you.

## Your next step

List every lead you are currently talking to and write down the next step for each one. If you struggle to remember where some conversations stand, that is a clear sign a simple CRM would help.
